CASE STUDY · O2 TECH AI · HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP WORKFLOW REDESIGN

01Brief | Core Idea and Method

In hardware supply chain data, mistakes are expensive and full automation is unrealistic. The real work is the balance between the AI agent and the human expert: a human-in-the-loop flow that makes heavy data visible, trusted and usable.

SNAPSHOT · SETUP↗ SNAPSHOT · CONFIRM PARTS↗

The study reads the real system: production source code and the live interface. The method is thick thinking. Walk the buyer's real scenarios, then hold the screens against dense B2B products like Palantir, Flexport and Ramp.

Three lenses: product mindset, user psychology, visual hierarchy. The goal is a rebuild with low cost and high return.

02Workflow | The Flow and the HITL Gate

O2 Tech AI processes a BOM in four stages:

01 Setup → 02 Confirm Parts → 03 Source & Price → 04 Build Quotes

  • Step 01 Setup: the buyer or engineer enters the hard rules. AVL policy, hard or soft. A target price ceiling. Packaging and a distributor allowlist.
  • Step 02 Confirm Parts: the core HITL gate. The agent parses the BOM, extracts standard parts, then pauses itself and asks a human to review and correct.

03Diagnosis | What Is Broken and Why

1. Product mindset: rules without attribution

  • Rules and results are disconnected. The user sets hard AVL, a price target and packaging rules in Setup. Then 6 lines arrive unpriced and the system never says why. Out of stock? Blocked by hard AVL? A wrong MPN? The user cannot tell.
  • Time and state conflict. 18 of 30 prices are over 21 hours old. Two refresh buttons sit side by side with equal weight, with no risk levels and no explanation. That invites wrong clicks and burns API cost.

2. User psychology: the trust gap

  • The re-check trap. The header shows results and task status but gives no reasoning. Users respond by re-checking every line, and the automation loses the savings it was hired for.
  • No lineage. Decisions show only results. There is no one-click view of the original part data next to the AI normalized data.
  • No undo, no feedback loop. Users cannot tell whether the system remembers a fix, whether one click can revert it, or whether an audit log exists. The interface answers none of this.

3. Visual hierarchy: data overload

  • A debug state in production. 28 columns open by default, no sticky columns. Nearly 40% of the width holds blanks or repeated constants. Part status hides behind horizontal scroll.
  • Hierarchy by shrinking type. Secondary information only gets smaller, so it gets hard to read. Colour, weight and grouping go unused.
  • No visualisation. A pure table, with no risk distribution and no breakdown of unpriced causes. The user gets no sense of global control.

04Ideation | Three Directions

The breakthrough is a trust ladder: expose the right information at the right depth, and balance cognitive load against confidence. I drew three directions:

Option A · Progressive attribution and lineage

  • Strategy: keep the table. Add micro attribution. A price opens its lineage one level deep, which supplier and which day. Edits keep the original text as a diff. One-click undo, and corrections flow back to the matcher.
  • Character: low build cost, small change for current users, aimed straight at the fear of trusting.

Option B · Exception-driven action queue

  • Strategy: hide the 80% of lines matched with high confidence. The screen becomes a queue of decision cards. Attention lands on the 6 nodes that need a human, one approval each, then the flow moves on.
  • Character: fastest handling and zero noise, and it demands high initial trust in the agent.

Option C · Co-pilot workspace

  • Strategy: a lean table on the left, an AI panel with a what-if simulator on the right. The user speaks plainly: refresh every stale Murata capacitor.
  • Character: the most flexible version and the most expensive. Heavy build, and heavy inference cost on every session.

05Success Metrics | What I Would Measure

Five numbers that measure the business value of the rebuild:

  1. Cycle time per BOM: average time from upload to exported quote.
  2. Spot-check ratio: how often users re-check lines the agent already resolved.
  3. Unpriced quote leakage: defective quotes that slip out because a blocked rule went unseen.
  4. Manual override rate: how often users correct the agent. It reads accuracy and learning.
  5. First-pass acceptance: BOMs that go straight through to Source and Price with no edits.

06Redesign Selection | The Choice and the Polish

The choice: option A, with option B's exception-first triage folded in. Hybrid, light.

  • The logic: an early stage B2B AI product should trade the lowest build cost and the lowest API cost for the highest customer trust and the fastest landing. A plus B changes no backend data structure and clears the trust and efficiency problems at the highest ROI.

WHERE THE POLISH GOES

  1. Build the trust ladder: every exception states its cause. The row says hard AVL blocked, or out of stock.
  2. Converge the table: lock 8 core columns, fold debug and rare columns into a drawer, and use semantic colour in place of broad highlight.
  3. Make change visible and reversible: hover shows the original text against the standard MPN, with row-level undo. Safety builds trust.
